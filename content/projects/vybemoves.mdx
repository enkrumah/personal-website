  title="Find a film from a feeling"
  className="mt-16 sm:mt-20"
/>

Vybemovies is a movie discovery product I built for the moment you know the mood and not the title. A normal search works when you already know the title, the actor, or the genre. It is much worse when you only know the feeling. It is live at [vybemoves.com](https://vybemoves.com).

People rarely start a movie night with the right query. They start with a limit, a tone, or something they want to avoid. The product has to understand that, find films that fit, and let taste change what comes next.

<SectionHeader
  id="problem"
  eyebrow="Problem"
  title="Lookup is not discovery"
  className="mt-16 sm:mt-20"
/>

Someone can know the experience they want and still have no title in mind.

<PullQuote>Something dark and cerebral, but not depressing.</PullQuote>

That sentence asks for a mood and rules something out. The request can also include themes, an era, pacing, or a genre, often several at once. A keyword search turns it into a broad bucket, such as thriller or drama, and misses the part that mattered.

<SectionHeader
  id="search-modes"
  eyebrow="Search strategy"
  title="Three ways to trade depth for speed"
  className="mt-16 sm:mt-20"
/>

The same query can take more than one path. Which path runs is a product decision: how much relevance is worth the latency and the cost before the system answers.

<SearchModes modes={searchModes} />

Quick Match, Best Match, and Explore All are the fast, smart, and hybrid paths. Quick Match searches for meaning and stops there, so it can answer quickly. Best Match takes those candidates and asks a language model to reorder them. Explore All keeps the meaning and adds catalog facts, filters, and the live catalog. The diagram is the part they share, and then the split.
